Approach

The input pairs are already rectified, so a point in the left image appears on the same row in the right image, shifted horizontally by its disparity d.

  1. For each pixel in the left image, take a 21 × 21 window around it.
  2. In the right image, start at the same pixel location and slide the window left along the row, up to 90 pixels, computing the sum of squared differences (SSD) at each position. We only search to the left because every point shifts left in the right image; capping the search at 90 pixels removed most false matches.
  3. The position with the lowest cost is the match, and the horizontal offset is the disparity.
  4. Convert disparity to depth with the camera's focal length f (3740 px) and baseline B (160 mm), plus the dataset's disparity offset.
    5. Z=fBd
  5. Back-project every pixel to 3D, color it from the left image, and render the point cloud.
