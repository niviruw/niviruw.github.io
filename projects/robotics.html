Tower of Hanoi

The arm solves Tower of Hanoi with three blocks and a suction-cup gripper. Each tower position and block height has two stored joint configurations: one touching the block and one just above it. Separate sets are stored for the simulator and the real robot. Every move follows the same pattern: go above the block, drop down, turn on suction, lift, move above the destination, lower, release, lift. After each grab, the program reads the gripper's suction sensor and stops if no block was picked up.

Forward kinematics

Given six joint angles, forward kinematics computes where the gripper ends up, using the product-of-exponentials formulation: each joint gets a screw axis Si from its rotation axis and a point on that axis, and M is the gripper's pose at the home position.

T(θ)= e[S1]θ1 e[S2]θ2 ⋯ e[S6]θ6 M

Other labs

  • Inverse kinematics. A closed-form, elbow-up solution that takes a target gripper position and yaw and solves for all six joint angles, checked with the forward kinematics above.
  • Vision-based pick and place. A camera finds colored blocks with HSV thresholding and blob detection. Their pixel locations are converted to world coordinates, and inverse kinematics picks them up and places them.
